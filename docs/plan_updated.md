2. Proposed flagship review paper
Recommended research direction
Can Published Evidence Support National-Scale Inference on Metal Contamination in Bangladesh's Rivers?
A Critical Systematic Review of Analytical Comparability, Spatial Representativeness, and Ecological Risk Assessment

Article type

Critical systematic review + systematic evidence map + methodological appraisal.

Geographical coverage

Bangladesh's river and estuarine systems, including the Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna delta.

Proposed literature window

January 2000 to the final search date in 2026.

Environmental matrices

River surface water and riverbed sediment, treated separately.

Primary contribution

Establish whether published observations, analytical practices and geographical coverage are sufficient to support reliable national assessments.

Five research questions
I would structure the entire paper around these five questions.

RQ1 — What evidence exists?

How have river-water and sediment heavy-metal observations evolved across Bangladesh between 2000 and 2026?

We investigate:
- Metals and metalloids reported.
- River systems and geographical distribution.
- Sampling years and seasonal coverage.
- Spatial and temporal changes in research attention.
- Concentration distributions by environmental matrix.
RQ2 — How reliable are the measurements?

How comparable are measurements reported by different studies?

This is potentially the most distinctive part of the paper.

We examine:
Analytical dimension	What we investigate
Instrumentation	Flame-AAS, GF-AAS, ICP-MS, ICP-OES and others
Digestion	Aqua regia, acid digestion, HF-based total digestion
Water fraction	Dissolved, filtered, unfiltered and total recoverable
Sediment preparation	Dry-weight basis, digestion protocols, particle-size treatment
Detection limits	LOD, LOQ and treatment of non-detects
Quality control	Certified reference materials, blanks, replicates and recovery
Reporting	Units, summary statistics, sampling location and metadata

The objective is to determine whether two reported concentrations are genuinely comparable or whether methodological differences make direct comparison scientifically questionable.

RQ3 — How representative is the geographical evidence?

Are the published studies representative of Bangladesh's river network?

Investigate:
- Concentration of studies around Dhaka and Chattogram.
- Underrepresented river basins.
- Repeated measurements at the same physical stations.
- Rivers without any published measurements.
- Availability and accuracy of geographical coordinates.
- Differences between major river corridors and peripheral tributaries.
Importantly, do not require exact GPS coordinates as an inclusion criterion for the systematic review. A publication with a documented river name but no station coordinates remains relevant to the evidence synthesis.

Instead, classify location precision:
1. Exact GPS coordinates.
2. Named and identifiable sampling station.
3. Identifiable river reach or locality.
4. River-level identification only.
5. Geographical location unresolved.
This prevents the geographical analysis from artificially excluding potentially relevant studies.

RQ4 — Are the environmental risk assessments scientifically comparable?

Evaluate the use of:
- Contamination Factor (CF).
- Geoaccumulation Index (\(I_{geo}\)).
- Pollution Load Index (PLI).
- Potential Ecological Risk Index (RI).
- Heavy Metal Pollution Index (HPI).
- Heavy Metal Evaluation Index (HEI).
- Hazard Quotients and related exposure indicators.
Investigate the sensitivity of results to background values, toxic response factors, concentration fractions and regulatory benchmarks.

A particularly useful contribution would be showing when different published studies use apparently identical risk indices but obtain results that are not directly comparable because their underlying assumptions differ.

RQ5 — What conclusions can actually be defended?

This is where the review should deliver its strongest contribution.

Identify:
- Which rivers have sufficient evidence for reliable contamination assessment?
- Which metals have consistent evidence of elevated concentrations?
- Which apparent pollution hotspots are supported by multiple independent studies?
- Which conclusions are driven by individual studies or repeated sampling locations?
- What evidence is insufficient for national-level generalization?
- What should a standardized Bangladesh river-monitoring programme measure?
The final output should be a scientifically justified roadmap, not simply a statement that more research is needed.

I would not commit to a meta-analysis in the title or protocol until the comparability of the available studies has been established.

Phase C — Build a new authoritative evidence database
This should become the main technical asset of the review.

I suggest creating four logically separate levels:
Study

DOI, authors, publication year, journal, study design

Sampling station

Station identifier, locality, coordinates, precision and river reach

Element, concentration, matrix, fraction, instrument, digestion, LOD and units

Phase D — Quality assessment
This section should be one of the defining contributions of the paper.

We need an explicit critical appraisal framework.

I suggest assessing studies across eight domains:
Domain	Assessment
Sampling design	Spatial coverage, sample size and replication
Temporal design	Seasonal and multi-year coverage
Geographical provenance	GPS precision and site identification
Sample handling	Filtration, preservation and preparation
Analytical method	Instrument and digestion suitability
Analytical QA/QC	Blanks, CRMs, replicates and recovery
Detection reporting	LOD, LOQ and non-detect treatment
Statistical reporting	Variability, independence and reproducibility

Classify the evidence as high, moderate, low or unclear for each applicable domain.

Do not simply add eight scores and declare a study scientifically reliable. The domains are not necessarily interchangeable, and a study can be strong analytically but weak geographically.

The CEE Critical Appraisal Tool and associated environmental evidence standards are useful methodological references for this work.

Phase E — Quantitative and geographical synthesis
This is where I would be disciplined.

Do not force a meta-analysis just because the article title contains the word meta-analysis.

I would use the following decision framework:
Dataset condition	Appropriate analysis
Compatible water concentrations and fractions	Separate quantitative concentration synthesis
Compatible sediment digestion and dry-weight measurements	Separate sediment synthesis
Reported study-level sample sizes and exceedance counts	Random-effects proportion analysis
Paired dry/wet measurements in the same studies	Within-study seasonal synthesis
Incompatible digestion or operational fractions	Stratified descriptive synthesis
Missing variance or insufficient study-level information	Structured narrative synthesis

Where pooling is defensible, use multilevel or random-effects approaches that account for study and sampling-site dependence.

For seasonal differences, prioritize paired within-study contrasts.

For concentration data with extreme right-skew and heterogeneous scales, evaluate log transformations and the suitability of geometric means versus arithmetic means.

Do not treat every row in an extracted CSV as an independent study participant.
Regulatory assessment
We must separate three regulatory questions:
1. Is the ambient river water compliant with its applicable surface-water standard?
2. Does raw river water exceed a drinking-water guideline used for screening?
3. What treatment or additional assessment would be required before using that water as a drinking-water source?
The WHO, Bangladesh ECR and irrigation thresholds should be compared in their appropriate contexts. Raw river-water exceedance of a potable standard must not be reported as automatic statutory non-compliance of the river itself.

The applicable regulatory versions must be checked at the review's search cutoff.
