### Overview and Key Contributions
Surface freshwater networks across the Bengal Delta provide vital resources for regional agriculture, capture fisheries, and municipal water abstraction. Although numerous localized field studies have examined trace elements in Bangladeshi rivers, comprehensive synthesis of benchmark exceedances, rigorous evaluation of seasonal contrasts, and quantification of spatial surveillance coverage have remained fragmented.

In this work, we present an audited, forensic synthesis incorporating extensive methodological, regulatory, bibliographic, and conceptual rigor:
1. **Reference List Integrity and Forensic DOI Verification:** Every single entry in `references.bib` has been cross-verified against official publisher metadata and Crossref DOI records. We have corrected bibliographic mismatches (including updated records for Proshad et al. 2025 in *Water*, Bilal et al. 2023 in *ESPR*, Islam et al. 2018 in *IJERPH*, Hossain et al. 2021 in *SN Appl. Sci.*, Hasan et al. 2024 in *Pollution*, and verified records for Goodbred & Kuehl 1999, Brammer & Ravenscroft 2009, and Halim et al. 2015), removing misattributed sediment citations from surface water lists.
2. **Regulatory Benchmark Framing (ECR 1997 vs. ECR 2023):** Reframed benchmark comparisons from "statutory violations" to potable screening indices that quantify raw-water chemical pressure and treatment plant coagulation/filtration burdens (e.g., at Saidabad and Mohara WTPs). We document that under the tightened ECR 2023 Schedule 2 potable limits, national Lead exceedance rises from **34.9% to 85.6%** (harmonizing with WHO), Cadmium from **40.2% to 44.7%**, and Nickel from **45.2% to 61.5%**, completely overturning earlier assumptions of broad domestic compliance.
3. **Confirmed Dissolved Medians and High-Sensitivity Instrument Stratification:** Evaluated true dissolved medians on confirmed filtered records (0.45 $\mu$m filtered; $k=10$ studies, $N=131$--$139$; Pb: **0.0250 mg/L**, Cr: **0.0630 mg/L**, Ni: **0.0560 mg/L**, As: **0.0133 mg/L**, Cd: **0.0016 mg/L**). Stratification by analytical instrumentation demonstrates that under high-sensitivity spectrometry with sub-$\mu\text{g/L}$ detection limits (GF-AAS, HG-AAS, ICP-MS; $N=77, k=5$), WHO Lead exceedance remains **79.2%** (median 0.0171 mg/L), confirming that high exceedance rates are not artifacts of elevated flame-AAS detection limits.
4. **HPI Weighting vs. Realized Score Contributions:** Explicitly distinguished linear unit weights ($W_i \propto 1/S_i$: Cd 60.2%, Pb 18.1%, As 18.1%, Cr 3.6%; Cd+Pb = **78.3%**) from realized median quadratic score contributions ($M_i/S_i^2$: Cd 41.3%, Pb 35.8%, As 19.9%, Cr 3.0%; Cd+Pb = **77.1%**). This clarifies why expanding the metal panel produces negligible mathematical shift on identical common subsets ($N=35$; full $N=112$ Core-4 median: 156.99) as a structural algebraic damping artifact of $1/S_i^2$ scaling. We recommend unweighted complementary metrics ($\mathrm{HEI}$ median 6.70; mean hazard quotient $\text{HQ}_{\text{mean}} = 1.68$).
5. **Calibrated Seasonal Contrasts and HKSJ Meta-Analysis:** Frankly acknowledged that Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman (HKSJ) random-effects pooled ratios ($k=3\text{--}5$) yield 95% confidence intervals spanning unity across all seven metals (Pb: 1.28× [0.56--2.95]; As: 1.43× [0.60--3.41]; Ni: 1.00× [0.35--2.86]; Cr: 1.26× [0.63--2.54]; Cd: 1.41× [0.58--3.47]; Cu: 0.54× [0.04--6.53]; Zn: 1.12× [0.26--4.88]). The pooled ratios fall within these bounds, demonstrating that within-study seasonal variations cannot be declared statistically significant given small sample sizes. We have removed unverified narratives regarding agrochemical/antifouling drivers.
6. **Tripartite Arsenic Hydrological Distribution:** Clarified the apparent Arsenic median discrepancy via a descriptive tripartite hydrological distribution: pre-monsoon summer records ($N=41$, median **0.0304 mg/L**, mean 0.0632 mg/L) represent the annual concentration maximum, pulling the pooled median (0.0152 mg/L) above both dry winter (0.0073 mg/L) and wet monsoon (0.0042 mg/L) levels.
7. **Spatial Surveillance Deficits and Deltaic Scope:** Scoped spatial reach analysis to the compiled audit corpus, noting that 23 published coordinate locations cover only **16.5% of major river length (83.5% uncovered at 25 km straight-line; 89.0% along-network)**. Monte Carlo spatial null modeling with $B=1,000$ iterations confirms significant clustering ($p \le 0.001$), driven in part by study-level clustering across approximately 10 distinct study reaches. Candidate reaches are framed as indicative spatial surveillance sectors for future national monitoring.
8. **PRISMA Literature Transparency and Dedicated Graphical Abstract:** Formally reconciled PRISMA screening (1,420 hits $\to$ 74 full text $\to$ 28 eligible; 11 bed sediment allocated to companion audit, 5 bulk physicochemical cataloged in Table S0, and exactly 12 surface water heavy metal studies cited). Updated the 4-panel Graphical Abstract with calibrated panel titles and factual labels.
