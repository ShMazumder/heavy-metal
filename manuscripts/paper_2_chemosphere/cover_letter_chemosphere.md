### Overview and Key Contributions
Surface freshwater networks across the Bengal Delta provide vital resources for regional agriculture, capture fisheries, and municipal water abstraction. Although numerous localized field studies have examined trace elements in Bangladeshi rivers, comprehensive synthesis of benchmark exceedances, rigorous evaluation of seasonal contrasts, and quantification of spatial surveillance coverage have remained fragmented.

In this revision, we incorporated extensive audit refinements addressing peer review feedback:
1. **Audited Multi-Study Compilation (2010--2024):** Audited 268 surface water observations (162 heavy metal records across 12 peer-reviewed field studies published between 2010 and 2024; 106 physicochemical-only records across 5 studies). An explicit audit log diffing raw entries against primary texts resolved historical transcription typos (e.g., P04 BS3 winter Pb 0.33 vs. 33.0 mg/L) and unit scaling errors (P27 Padma metals $\mu\text{g/L} \to \text{mg/L}$).
2. **Benchmark Exceedances with Clustered Uncertainty:** Evaluated concentrations against WHO reference drinking guidelines (4th ed., 2022 addenda), Bangladesh ECR statutory standards, and FAO irrigation benchmarks using 5,000-resample study-level cluster bootstrapping. Lead (Pb) exceeded the WHO guideline (0.010 mg/L) in **85.6%** of records (study-level cluster 95% CI: 70.6--94.1%, unadjusted Wilson CI: 79.0--90.4%, median: 0.0286 mg/L). Total Chromium (60.6% [32.3--82.9%]), Nickel (54.8% [22.4--79.1%]), Arsenic (54.9% [18.1--84.9%]), and Cadmium (44.7% [13.5--77.1%]) also exhibited substantial WHO exceedances.
3. **Hotspot Sensitivity Analysis:** Excluding Greater Dhaka's peri-urban industrial rivers (Buriganga, Turag, Shitalakhya) showed lower exceedances (e.g., Bangladesh standard exceedance for Pb fell from 34.9% to 18.3%, and Arsenic from 18.0% to 8.9%), empirically demonstrating that literature-derived metrics are strongly shaped by metropolitan industrial outfalls.
4. **Standardized Fixed-Panel Pollution Indices:** Evaluated the Heavy Metal Pollution Index ($\mathrm{HPI}$) on a standardized Core-4 panel (As, Cd, Cr, Pb; $N=112$), yielding a median score of **156.99** (IQR: 280.55, mean: 395.21, max: 2,682.05), with 58.9% exceeding Prasad and Bose's critical limit of 100. Core-4 $\mathrm{HPI}$ demonstrated strong rank concordance with unweighted $\mathrm{HEI}$ ($\rho = 0.976, p < 10^{-15}$).
5. **Seasonal Contrasts with Matched-Site Controls:** Two-sided Mann-Whitney U hypothesis testing with family-wise Holm-Bonferroni correction confirmed higher dry-season Arsenic in pooled records (dry median 0.0073 mg/L vs. wet median 0.0042 mg/L; 1.74× ratio, $p = 0.0039$, $p_{\mathrm{adj}} = 0.0273$). Within-study paired contrasts on matched sampling sites ($k=4$, median ratio 1.21×, $p = 0.3750$) demonstrated limited statistical power under clustering, consistent with competing mechanisms of monsoonal volumetric dilution versus dry-season geogenic baseflow/irrigation return flows. Lead and Nickel exhibited no significant seasonal contrast.
6. **River Network Coverage and GIS Mapping:** Straight-line and topological along-network stream distance routing across HydroSHEDS HydroRIVERS established that **88.0% of major river reach length (7,369.3 km across 3,407 reaches)** lies $>25$ km straight-line (and 94.6% along-network) from published sampling locations. We provide an publication GIS coverage map and 15 prioritized, geographically dispersed surveillance candidate reaches with gazetteer-verified administrative names.
