### Overview and Novelty
River waters across the Bengal Delta provide freshwater for over 170 million people, yet surface waters face unprecedented chemical pollution from expanding industrial corridors. Despite numerous localized studies, comprehensive national-scale audits of regulatory non-compliance, seasonal dilution dynamics, and spatial monitoring network design remain entirely unaddressed.

In this study, we present a nationwide synthesis and risk evaluation of dissolved heavy metals across Bangladesh river waters:
1. **Pervasive Drinking Benchmark Violations:** Auditing 268 surface water records across 12 river systems against WHO guidelines (4th ed.) and Bangladesh ECR 2023 standards revealed alarming public health failures: **87.7% of all sampled river waters violate WHO drinking safety limits for Lead (Pb)**, while Chromium (62.5%), Nickel (57.7%), and Arsenic (54.9%) exhibit chronic non-compliance.
2. **Critical Multi-Metal Toxicity:** The Heavy Metal Pollution Index (HPI) classified **51.6%** of multi-metal samples into the **Critical Pollution category ($HPI > 200$)**, with an extreme mean $HPI$ of 1,483.1 (critical threshold = 100).
3. **Statistical Proof of Monsoon Dilution:** Non-parametric Mann-Whitney U hypothesis testing confirmed that monsoon hydraulic discharge dilutes Chromium (1.99×, $p=0.0191$), Cadmium (1.81×, $p=0.0204$), Arsenic (1.74×, $p=0.0021$), and Zinc (7.31×, $p=0.0458$) by approximately 50% compared to winter baseflow conditions. Conversely, Lead displayed no significant seasonal dilution ($p=0.4637$), proving continuous wet-season non-point diffuse urban stormwater wash-off.
4. **Geospatial Monitoring Network Optimization:** Using HydroSHEDS HydroRIVERS topology, we analyzed 4,435 major unmonitored reaches and established an evidence-based ranking of the top 15 priority GPS coordinates for national monitoring station expansion by the Department of Environment (DoE) and Bangladesh Water Development Board (BWDB), identifying the lower Meghna confluence at Chandpur as the primary national blind spot.
