### Overview and Key Contributions
Surface freshwater networks across the Bengal Delta provide vital resources for regional agriculture, capture fisheries, and municipal water abstraction. Although numerous localized field studies have examined trace elements in Bangladeshi rivers, comprehensive synthesis of benchmark exceedances, rigorous evaluation of seasonal contrasts, and quantification of spatial surveillance coverage have remained fragmented.

In this revision (Round 3), we have systematically addressed all reviewer remarks and incorporated extensive methodological, spatial, and factual refinements:
1. **Audited Multi-Study Compilation (2010--2024):** Audited 268 surface water observations (162 heavy metal records across 12 peer-reviewed field studies published between 2010 and 2024; 106 physicochemical-only records across 5 studies). We provide a complete study reconciliation table (`supplement_s0_study_reconciliation.csv`) documenting all 28 candidate studies and a sampling station provenance catalog (`supplement_s2_site_provenance.csv`) documenting all 23 unique coordinates with GPS vs. reach-centroid geocoding and precision classifications.
2. **Benchmark Exceedances with Clustered Uncertainty:** Evaluated concentrations against WHO reference drinking guidelines (4th ed., 2022 addenda), Bangladesh ECR statutory standards, and FAO irrigation benchmarks using 5,000-resample study-level cluster bootstrapping and DerSimonian-Laird random-effects meta-analytic pooling. Lead (Pb) exceeded the WHO guideline (0.010 mg/L) in **85.6%** of records (study-level cluster 95% CI: 70.5--94.4%, unadjusted Wilson CI: 79.1--90.4%, random-effects pooled estimate: 79.9% [95% CI: 57.4--92.2%], median: 0.0286 mg/L). Total Chromium (60.6% [32.3--82.9%]), Nickel (54.8% [22.4--79.1%]), Arsenic (54.9% [18.1--84.9%]), and Cadmium (44.7% [13.5--77.1%]) also exhibited substantial WHO exceedances.
3. **Hotspot Sensitivity Analysis:** Excluding Greater Dhaka's peri-urban industrial rivers (Buriganga, Turag, Shitalakhya) showed lower exceedances under domestic standards (Bangladesh standard exceedance for Pb fell from 34.9% to 18.3%, and Arsenic from 18.0% to 8.9%), whereas WHO guideline exceedances remained elevated (82.6% for Pb, 66.7% for Cr), demonstrating that literature-derived metrics are strongly shaped by metropolitan industrial outfalls while broad trace element contamination is documented nationally.
4. **Standardized Fixed-Panel Pollution Indices:** Evaluated the Heavy Metal Pollution Index ($\mathrm{HPI}$) on a standardized Core-4 panel (As, Cd, Cr, Pb; $N=112$), yielding a median score of **156.99** (IQR: 280.55, mean: 395.21, max: 2,682.05), with 58.9% exceeding Prasad and Bose's critical limit of 100. Evaluating panels across identical common subsets ($N=35$) confirmed index mathematical invariance across Core-4 (254.1), Core-5 (253.4), and All-7 (253.0) panels. Unweighted Heavy Metal Evaluation Index ($\mathrm{HEI}$) yielded an unweighted mean hazard quotient of $\text{HQ}_{\text{mean}} = \text{HEI}/4 = 1.68$.
5. **Seasonal Contrasts with Matched-Site Controls:** Pooled non-parametric tests identified higher dry-season Arsenic (dry median 0.0073 mg/L vs. wet median 0.0042 mg/L; 1.74× ratio, $p = 0.0039$, Holm $p_{\mathrm{adj}} = 0.0273$). Within-study random-effects meta-analyses across investigations sampling matched river stations ($k=4$) confirmed dry-to-wet ratios $\ge 1.0$ across all seven metals (As: 1.43×, Pb: 1.28×, Cr: 1.26×), supporting competing hydrochemical hypotheses of monsoonal volumetric dilution versus dry-season geogenic baseflow and irrigation return flow.
6. **River Network Coverage and GIS Mapping:** Topological along-network stream distance routing across HydroSHEDS HydroRIVERS strictly clipped to Bangladesh borders (2,224 major reaches totaling 4,599.1 km) established that published stations cover only **16.5% of major river length (757.2 km; 83.5% / 3,841.9 km uncovered at 25 km straight-line; 89.0% / 4,094.5 km along-network)**. A 1,000-iteration spatial Monte Carlo null model established that observed coverage is significantly lower than the 38.1% expected under random station placement ($p < 0.0001$, 95% CI: 31.6--44.0%), confirming severe geographic clustering in academic surveillance. We provide a publication GIS coverage map and 15 prioritized, geographically dispersed surveillance candidate reaches with gazetteer-verified administrative names.
7. **Factual and Historical Corrections:** Accurately detailed Dhaka WASA water treatment plant configurations (Saidabad WTP Phases I--II abstracting from Sitalakhya; Phase III planned for Meghna; Padma-Jashaldia WTP treating Padma water since 2019) and clarified the timeline of tetraethyl lead phase-out in Bangladesh (1999) alongside current lead battery recycling contributions.
