### Context, Motivation, and Novelty
The river networks of the Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna (GBM) delta sustain over 170 million people but receive extensive untreated municipal and industrial effluents. While dozens of field monitoring studies have evaluated heavy metals across localized river channels over the past fifteen years, the published literature remains fragmented. Two recent general reviews (Pandit et al., 2025; and a December 2025 multi-matrix synthesis) compiled broad concentration ranges across Bangladesh, yet both treated published datasets as a methodologically homogeneous whole. 

Our critical systematic review and evidence map—conducted in strict compliance with the **PRISMA 2020** and **ROSES** standards—delivers the first forensic appraisal of whether the published scientific evidence base is methodologically sound, analytically comparable, and geographically representative enough to support national environmental inference. 

Auditing 28 peer-reviewed investigations (557 verified chemical assays across 12 river systems) across eight CEE methodological domains, we uncover three profound vulnerabilities in the published literature:
1. **Analytical Incomparability and LOD Censoring:** Flame-AAS studies (deployed in 48.8% of water assays) report median concentrations 2.0$\times$ to 6.7$\times$ higher than high-sensitivity GF-AAS or ICP-MS benchmarks (e.g., Pb median: 0.110 vs. 0.0171 mg/L, $p < 0.001$). We show that this divergence is an artifact of high flame detection limits (0.010–0.050 mg/L) combined with non-detect censoring (46.4% of studies omit LOD definitions or handle non-detects non-transparently). Furthermore, 41.3% of water assays fail to verify 0.45 $\mu\text{m}$ filtration, confounding suspended mineral particulates with dissolved bioavailable ions. Certified Reference Materials (CRMs) were omitted in 35.7% of studies.
2. **Surveillance Blind Spots (HydroSHEDS Routing):** Auditing 426 registered station entries demonstrates that 48.4% ($n=206$) lack coordinates entirely. Snapping verified stations to the HydroSHEDS HydroRIVERS topological network (2,224 major reaches, 4,599.1 km) reveals that empirical sampling covers only **16.5% of major river corridor length** at 25 km (11.0% along-network stream distance), statistically indistinguishable from severe spatial clustering (Monte Carlo null model $p \le 0.001$). The vast transboundary corridors of the Padma (Ganges), Jamuna (Brahmaputra), and Lower Meghna remain virtually unstudied.
3. **Mathematical Resolution of Index Weighting Damping:** We derive the algebraic proof demonstrating that the widely used Heavy Metal Pollution Index ($\mathrm{HPI}$) scales with inverse standards squared ($1/S_i^2$). This concentrates **78.3% of linear weights and 77.1% of realized index scores** onto Cadmium and Lead, rendering the inclusion of other metals mathematically inert and explaining the false "panel invariance" reported across South Asian literature.
4. **Statutory Realignment Under ECR 2023 Schedule 2:** Auditing historical compliance against Bangladesh's newly gazetted *Environmental Conservation Rules 2023* (which lowered the potable Lead limit from 0.050 to 0.010 mg/L), statutory drinking benchmark exceedance surges from 34.9% to **85.6%**, confirming severe raw-water chemical stress on municipal water supply intakes (e.g., Dhaka WASA's Saidabad WTP).
5. **Actionable Evidence-Readiness Framework:** We deliver a standardized evidence-readiness protocol and a reach-prioritized 15-sector blueprint for institutional deployment by the Department of Environment (DoE) and Bangladesh Water Development Board (BWDB).
