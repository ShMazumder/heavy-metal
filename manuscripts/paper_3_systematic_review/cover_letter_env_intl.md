### Executive Summary & Novel Contributions
River networks within the Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna (GBM) delta sustain over 170 million people but experience extreme industrial effluent loads and agrochemical contamination. Despite hundreds of published sampling surveys, environmental policy has been paralyzed by conflicting reports, methodological artifacts, and geographic clustering. In this manuscript, prepared in strict compliance with the **PRISMA 2020** framework, we synthesize 28 peer-reviewed studies (557 validated assays) to deliver four decisive scientific contributions:

1. **National Statutory Realignment (ECR 2023):** We provide the first comprehensive compliance audit following the enactment of the Bangladesh *Environmental Conservation Rules 2023* Schedule 2. We demonstrate that under tightened national limits (e.g., Pb reduced from 0.050 to 0.010 mg/L), drinking water benchmark exceedance surges from 34.9% to **85.6%**, harmonizing with WHO guidelines and defining urgent raw-water treatment burdens for metropolitan municipal intakes.
2. **Multivariable Meta-Regression (2,000 Cluster Bootstraps):** We quantify the physical and seasonal drivers of trace metal exceedance. Low-flow winter baseflow significantly triples the odds of Chromium exceedance ($\mathrm{OR} = 2.77$ [1.15–28.54], $p = 0.026$). Pre-monsoon dry season dynamics intensify Arsenic exceedances ($\mathrm{OR} = 5.67$), driven by intensive *boro* rice aquifer pumping discharging into low-flow channels.
3. **Algebraic Resolution of HPI Mathematical Damping:** We derive the analytical proof showing that the widely used Heavy Metal Pollution Index ($\mathrm{HPI}$) scales with inverse standards squared ($1/S_i^2$). This concentrates **78.3% of index weight onto Cadmium and Lead**, rendering multi-element additions mathematically inert and explaining the persistent "panel stability" reported across South Asian literature.
4. **Hydrographic Network Blind Spot Cartography:** Snapping sampling stations to HydroSHEDS reach topology reveals that published academic monitoring covers only **16.5% of major river length (757.2 km / 4,599.1 km)** at 25 km ($p \le 0.001$), leaving 83.5% of major river corridors completely unmonitored.
